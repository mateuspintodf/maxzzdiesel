Technical guide

Bosch CRIN 4.2 (Detroit DD15) Rebuild Guide: How to Disassemble Without Breaking Solenoid Pins, Critical Measurements & Part Numbers

By the Maxzz Diesel technical team. Technical review: Waldyr Mazzer (40+ years in diesel injection, former Bosch and Delphi senior instructor) and Arthur Bonachella (Robiel and Diesel Tech Academy instructor).

Direct answer

A Bosch CRIN 4.2 injector from a Detroit DD13, DD15 or DD16 is rebuilt by testing it first, releasing the solenoid assembly without twisting it against its pins, cleaning and measuring every component, replacing the control valve, nozzle and seals that fail inspection, then validating the result on a calibrated bench with ISO 4113 fluid.

Why the DD15 injector is rebuilt at component level

The Detroit DD13, DD15 and DD16 use Bosch common rail injectors, and the injector is the part of the fuel system most often rebuilt rather than replaced. A rebuild keeps the injector body, which carries the machined bores and the electrical connector, and replaces the parts that wear: the nozzle, the control valve, the shims and the seals.

The risk in a CRIN 4.2 rebuild is mechanical, not hydraulic. The solenoid connects to the body through fine pins, and a solenoid that is twisted, pried or pulled off-axis breaks them. A broken pin turns a repairable injector into scrap. This guide follows the order of work taught in the Diesel Tech Academy course for this injector.

Before disassembly: test and record

Never open an injector you have not tested. A bench test with ISO 4113 calibration fluid tells you whether the fault is electrical, hydraulic or in the nozzle, and it gives you a reference to compare with after the rebuild. Record the injector part number from the label, because the control valve and nozzle you order depend on it.

The checks below are the ones we run in this order. Reference values come from the Bosch data for the exact injector number, so we list the check and what it tells you rather than a single number that would be wrong for another variant.

CheckInstrumentWhat it tells you
Coil resistance and continuityMultimeter or injector coil testerOpen or shorted solenoid, damaged pin connection
Insulation to groundInsulation testerLeakage through the solenoid winding to the body
Delivery and leak-off at test pointsCommon rail test bench, ISO 4113 fluidWorn control valve, nozzle leakage, sticking needle
Visual inspection of the connector and pinsMagnifierBent or cracked pins before any tool touches the injector

Disassembly without breaking the solenoid pins

Clamp the injector in a holder that supports the body and never grip the solenoid or the connector. Release the retaining parts in the order given in the Robiel CRIN 4.2 tool set, then lift the solenoid assembly straight along its axis. If it does not come free with light, even force, stop and check what is still holding it. Prying is what breaks pins.

Keep the parts of each injector in separate trays. Shims and control valve parts are matched to one injector, and mixing them between cylinders is the most common reason a rebuilt set will not balance on the bench.

Critical measurements

After cleaning, measure rather than guess. The table lists the dimensions that decide whether a part is reused. Use the Bosch data for the exact injector for the limits.

ComponentMeasureDecision
Control valve seat and sealing faceWear, scoring, flatnessReplace the valve when the seat is marked
Nozzle needle and bodyNeedle guide clearance, seat conditionReplace the nozzle when it fails the leak or flow test
Solenoid armature gap and shimsGap against the specified valueSelect the shim that restores the specified gap
Sealing faces and O-ring groovesDamage or corrosionReplace the seals, polish only what the tool set allows

Cleaning, assembly and validation

Clean metal components in an ultrasonic bath with Maxzz Fluid Series 2271 Sonic and rinse with 2221S Solv. Assemble in a clean area, torque to the Bosch specification, and test on a common rail bench with ISO 4113 calibration fluid (Maxzz 4113 CAL). A rebuilt injector should match the delivery of its set within the tolerance for the test points before it goes back to the engine.

Frequently asked questions

Can the solenoid of a CRIN 4.2 be reused?

Yes, if it passes the coil resistance, continuity and insulation checks and the pins are intact. A damaged pin cannot be repaired reliably.

Which tool set do I need?

Robiel 323.50.40 is the tool set listed for the Bosch CRIN 4.2 injector.

Is the course hands-on?

Yes. The Diesel Tech Academy Bosch CRIN 4.2 course teaches teardown without breaking solenoid pins, assembly and validation.
